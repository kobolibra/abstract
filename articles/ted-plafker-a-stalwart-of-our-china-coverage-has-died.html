Ted Plafker, a stalwart of our China coverage, has died

He was a giant among Western journalists in the country

Nov 12th 2026

China does not make reporting easy for foreign correspondents from the West. They are often harassed by local authorities when doing their jobs. They are routinely warned against reporting that offends the Communist Party. Resident visas can be hard to obtain. It is a difficult country for any Western journalist truly to call home.

Yet Ted Plafker, a native of New Jersey, did so. He arrived in China in 1989 after a bike trip through Europe and Asia. He planned to stay a few weeks and return to America to find work as a journalist. He ended up staying, witnessing that year’s upheaval in Tiananmen Square and meeting Roberta, who was to become his wife and mother to their three children. He wrote for several publications, including, from 1994, The Economist. He joined the newspaper’s bureau in Beijing shortly after it opened in 1997 and was its longest-serving member.

Ted became fluent in Mandarin (on top of Russian, which he had mastered already as a student at Wesleyan University in Connecticut) and a giant among Beijing-based journalists—for three years he headed the Foreign Correspondents’ Club of China (the government shuns it). He will be missed for his great wisdom on the country, as well as his unfailing kindness, helpfulness and good humour.■
