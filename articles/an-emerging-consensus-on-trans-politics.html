An emerging consensus on trans politics

Voters want neither trans kids playing girls’ sports, nor politicians obsessed with it

Nov 12th 2026

The most effective Republican ad of 2024, many strategists agreed, was one that declared that “Kamala is for they/them” and Donald Trump was “for you”. This year, conservative groups tried to repeat the trick, devoting at least $183m to attack ads on transgender issues, according to AdImpact, a research firm. The strategy failed to protect Republican seats. Yet where trans-related issues were put to voters directly, the response was different.

Four states considered ballot measures to restrict girls’ and women’s sports to biological females. All passed, including in solidly Democratic states. That points to a new phase in the fight over trans rights. A growing share of voters don’t want trans kids playing girls’ sports. Nor do they want politicians to focus on the issue.

Two-thirds of Americans favour laws requiring athletes to play on teams that match their sex at birth, up from 58% in 2022, according to the Pew Research Centre. Around 30 states already have rules on trans athletes. In June the Supreme Court upheld such policies. Some 59% of adults approved of that ruling, almost twice the share who disapproved.

Though all the ballot measures this year concerned gender and sports, the specifics of the initiatives in Arizona, Colorado, Nebraska and Washington varied. Arizona’s includes requirements that trans people use bathrooms at schools and sports facilities that match their sex at birth. Washington’s measure, passed by a 12-point margin, requires medical documentation to verify sex for each child seeking to play girls’ sports.

Even as a majority opinion emerges on trans sports, it is a deciding issue for vanishingly few Americans. In Washington state, the trans measure passed despite Democratic congressional candidates winning nearly two-thirds of the statewide vote. A poll by AP-NORC in October found that just 11% of voters considered trans issues a top priority.

“After 2024 voters really thought that Democrats were overly preoccupied with these ‘social issues’,” says Tré Easton of Searchlight Institute, a Democrat-aligned think-tank. In this election, with voters concerned about affordability, it was Republicans who were caught in their own culture-war traps.

In a sign of shifting politics, James Talarico, the incoming Democratic senator from Texas, once called God “non-binary” but said during his campaign that he opposes “trans athletes playing in girls’ sports if it endangers safety or fairness.” Sherrod Brown, Ohio’s Senator-elect, took out his own ads in response to Republicans’ trans attacks during the campaign. “With everything going on,” his ads asked, “that’s what they are talking about?”■
